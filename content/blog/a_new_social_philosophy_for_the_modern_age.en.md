## The fit between governance and communication mediums

A system of government must fit not only human temperament and the human
condition, but also the prevailing communication medium of its era. A system
that works well in one communication age can unravel in another.

- In antiquity, democracy was strictly confined to city-states, because the
  city was the largest geographic and social scale at which direct
  communication and communal decision-making remained functional.
- As societies expanded, representative democracy became feasible only with
  mass communication technologies that could broadcast across nations.

## Centralized mass media and the illusion of stability

Until the advent of the internet, modern democracy functioned within a
centralized, one-to-many communication environment:

- Information flowed outward from a very small number of centralized hubs — a
  few major newspapers, select television channels and major networks,
  typically financed by concentrated capital.
- With low variance in the information broadcast to the public, messaging
  remained centered around a broad consensus. The system appeared functional
  and orderly, concealing deep structural vulnerabilities.

## The internet and the exposure of human nature

The decentralized internet broke this centralized filter, laying bare
uncomfortable truths about human cognition and behavior:

- **People are inherently stupid and irrational.** Contrary to democratic
  idealism, the broader populace does not operate through reasoned
  deliberation or balanced logic.
- **Tribalism over truth.** People are deeply bound to their tribal loyalties.
  When confronted with facts that contradict their camp or their pet beliefs,
  they reflexively reject them or invent flaws to discredit them. Conversely,
  they uncritically accept whatever aligns with their existing biases or makes
  them feel good.

## The flaw of adversarial systems

Modern democratic structures are fundamentally adversarial, usually
crystallizing into two main opposing camps or coalitions:

- This binary conflict dramatically intensifies tribalism rather than
  moderating it.
- It eliminates cognitive flexibility, preventing citizens from recognizing or
  adopting sensible ideas proposed by the opposing faction.
- Voters routinely select leaders based on superficial traits — such as
  physical stature or charisma — rather than on a substantive analysis of
  policy trade-offs.

## The failure of political science and social philosophy

Political theory and philosophy have long perpetuated the myth that citizens
are rational actors:

- Because the democratic system appeared to function under centralized
  broadcast media, political scientists saw no need to question it, and
  advertised the notion that voters weigh the advantages and disadvantages of
  competing policies.
- Major philosophical traditions — from Immanuel Kant onward — have refused to
  place human stupidity at the center of their models, seeking social remedies
  without confronting the real cognitive limitations of the masses.

## The imperative: a new philosophy and a new design of government

To move forward, we must address these realities head-on:

1. **Establish a new social philosophy.** A philosophical framework whose
   foundational assumption is that human beings are inherently stupid,
   irrational and tribal.
2. **Design a system for the reality of the masses.** While the exact
   mechanics remain to be invented, we must actively design a new system of
   governance engineered specifically to withstand and accommodate human
   irrationality, rather than relying on the naive expectation of an
   enlightened, rational citizenry.
