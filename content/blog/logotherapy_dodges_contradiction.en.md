Viktor Frankl's logotherapy holds that meaning is the route out of despair. Find meaning — including meaning *in* suffering — and you can endure almost anything. It is a serious position, developed under conditions that entitle it to respect, and it plainly helps people.

I still think it evades the central problem, and the evasion is the same one religion makes.

## The Flaw

Science shows us no meaning. There is no purpose written into the universe, no role assigned to us, nothing that our lives are *for* in any sense external to ourselves. This is the standard naturalist position and I take it to be correct.

So how does the prescription work?

Frankl is right that supplying a person with purpose lifts them out of depression. The clinical observation is sound. But look at what is happening: **we manufacture meaning and then inflate its importance far beyond what it is.**

The purposes we assign ourselves are modest — a project, a relationship, a craft, a duty. In the frame the universe actually presents, they do not matter. Treating them as though they do is not a discovery. It is a useful fiction, and the therapy works by helping the patient hold the fiction more firmly.

## The Contradiction We Have to Live Inside

I do not think this makes the fiction avoidable. That is what makes it interesting rather than merely a criticism.

**We must in some sense fool ourselves — inflate our own significance — while simultaneously remembering that no such significance exists.** Both halves are required. Drop the first and you get paralysis. Drop the second and you get religion.

That is the actual human condition, and I think it has to be faced directly rather than resolved. It is not a puzzle with a solution; it is a tension to be held.

## Why Evasion Fails

And here is my objection to logotherapy as a method.

To genuinely escape despair, the contradiction must be confronted rather than smoothed over. Religion smooths it over by supplying external meaning — the purpose is real, assigned by God, and no self-deception is involved. Logotherapy smooths it over in a subtler way: it supplies meaning without quite saying that the meaning is manufactured.

**Self-deception does not hold.** It surfaces. And I suspect it is precisely this suppressed knowledge — the half-recognition that the purpose was invented — that erupts later as depression and its relatives. You can fool yourself for a while. In some people it breaks, and it breaks worse for having been suppressed rather than acknowledged.

## Being Fair to Frankl

The honest version of the objection has to concede something.

Logotherapy *does* acknowledge that the meanings are self-assigned. Frankl is explicit that meaning is found or chosen rather than received, and that is a real difference from religion — it starts from fewer false assumptions and does not require believing anything untrue about the universe.

What it does not acknowledge is that a great deal of the suffering comes *from* their being self-assigned. The patient who half-knows his purpose is his own invention is in a different position from one who believes it was handed down, and the therapy does not address that difference. It treats the manufactured meaning as though it were simply meaning.

**It is better than religion. It shares the structure.**

## What I Would Want Instead

An approach that says plainly: there is no meaning, you will have to make one, the one you make will be genuinely arbitrary, and you will have to hold it anyway — knowing all of that.

That sounds harsher and I think it is actually gentler, because it does not require maintaining anything against the evidence. What breaks people is not the absence of cosmic purpose. It is the effort of not noticing that it is absent while depending on the pretence that it is not.
