If you wanted to demonstrate that your account of the world was genuinely tracking reality rather than merely describing it after the fact, there is one test that settles the matter beyond argument. State, in advance and in detail, something nobody has observed yet. Commit to it publicly. Then let the universe answer.

This is the hardest thing any system of thought can be asked to do, and it is the only real miracle on offer. Science does it routinely, at a level of precision that ought to astonish people far more than it does. Religion has never done it once.

## The Gaps in the Table

In 1869 Mendeleev arranged the known elements by atomic weight and found the pattern had holes in it. Rather than fudge the arrangement to close them, he did something extraordinary: he asserted that the holes were elements nobody had ever seen, and he described them.

He specified, for the element he called eka-silicon, an atomic weight of about 72, a density around 5.5 grams per cubic centimetre, a grey metallic appearance, an oxide of a particular formula and density, and a chloride that would boil below 100°C. He was describing a substance that no human being had encountered, from a gap in a table.

Fifteen years later Clemens Winkler isolated germanium. Atomic weight 72.6. Density 5.35. Grey metal. The oxide and the chloride came out as specified, the chloride boiling at 86°C.

Sit with the strangeness of that. A pattern in a list of numbers told a man in Russia the boiling point of a compound of an element that had not been discovered. The universe was under no obligation to comply. It complied.

## This Is Not an Isolated Trick

The Mendeleev case is famous, but it is representative rather than exceptional.

**Neptune** was found because the orbit of Uranus was slightly wrong. Le Verrier calculated where an unseen planet would have to be to produce the discrepancy and sent the coordinates to an observatory. Johann Galle found it that night, within a degree of the prediction. A planet located by arithmetic before anyone looked.

**General relativity** predicted that gravity bends starlight, by a specific amount — twice the value Newtonian physics allowed. In 1919 Eddington's eclipse expedition measured it. The same theory predicted that clocks run slower in stronger gravitational fields, which is now a routine engineering correction: GPS satellites would accumulate errors of about ten kilometres per day if the relativistic correction were omitted. Every phone navigating by satellite is a continuous experimental confirmation.

**The cosmic microwave background** was predicted as the cooled remnant of a hot early universe, with an estimated temperature, before anyone detected it. Penzias and Wilson found it in 1965 while trying to eliminate noise from an antenna. It is there, at 2.7 kelvin, filling all of space, exactly as a theory about the first moments of the universe said it would be.

**The Higgs boson** was proposed in 1964 to explain why particles have mass, and its properties constrained the energies at which it could be found. Nearly fifty years later, a machine built specifically to look in the predicted range found it. Half a century between the prediction and the confirmation, and the universe still had the particle where the mathematics said.

**Antimatter** came out of Dirac's equation as a solution he could not discard. He concluded that a positively charged twin of the electron must exist. Anderson found the positron four years later. It exists because an equation had two roots.

## What the Other Side Has

Now ask what religion has ever produced that answers to this description.

Two thousand years of theology, thousands of scriptures, an immense investment of the best minds of many centuries — and there is not one entry. No sacred text stated in advance the age of the earth, the mechanism of inheritance, the existence of an undiscovered continent, the cause of a disease, or the value of any physical constant. Not one theological system predicted a single fact about the world that was subsequently confirmed by anyone who did not already believe.

What religion offers instead falls into three categories, and none of them qualifies.

**Retrodiction.** The text is reinterpreted after the discovery to have hinted at it all along. Once we knew the universe had a beginning, "in the beginning" was recast as an anticipation of Big Bang cosmology. But this was never extracted from the text beforehand — and the same verses were, for centuries, read as supporting a young earth and a solid dome overhead. A text that can be reconciled with any finding after the fact has predicted nothing. It has been fitted.

**Prophecy of the unfalsifiable kind.** Wars will occur. Empires will fall. There will be earthquakes and false teachers and a time of trouble. These are not predictions; they are descriptions of every century in human history. Contrast them with "density 5.5, chloride boiling below 100°C." One of these can fail. That is the entire difference.

**Prophecy that failed.** Where religious predictions have been specific, they have been wrong, and the record is not obscure. The end of the world has been dated confidently and repeatedly for two millennia by people who were certain. Every date has passed. The response has never been to abandon the framework, only to recalculate — which is itself the tell.

## Why the Asymmetry Is Structural

It would be a mistake to treat this as a scoring exercise, as if religion had simply been unlucky. The asymmetry follows from what the two enterprises are.

A predictive claim is a wager. To make one you must possess a model with parts that constrain each other, so that fixing some of them fixes the rest whether you like the answer or not. Mendeleev could not choose germanium's density; the table chose it. Dirac could not choose whether the positron existed; the equation chose. That is what it means to have a model of a mechanism rather than a story about it: the model can force you to conclusions you did not want and did not expect.

Theology has no such machinery. Its central claim is an agent of unlimited power and undisclosed intentions, and an unconstrained agent constrains nothing. Any observation whatsoever is compatible with the will of such a being. That flexibility is presented as strength — the framework can accommodate anything — but accommodating anything is precisely why it can forecast nothing. A theory that forbids no outcome cannot be surprised by any outcome, and cannot earn credit from any either.

## The Miracle That Actually Happens

There is a real wonder here, and it is regularly missed because it has become ordinary.

Human beings, working with symbols on paper, have repeatedly determined facts about parts of reality they had never touched, seen, or visited — the interior of stars, the first minutes of the universe, particles too small and too short-lived to observe directly, elements that did not yet exist in any laboratory. They wrote down what they expected in advance. Then they looked, and it was there.

That is not a metaphor for magic. It is the thing itself: knowledge of the unseen, obtained by a method, verified by checking. It happens on a Tuesday, in a laboratory, and it works whether or not you believe in it — which is more than can be said for any of the alternatives that have claimed the word.
