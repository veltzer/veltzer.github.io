People have always wanted freedom, and the word covers several different things — economic freedom, political freedom, personal latitude. These are usually discussed as though they were separate goods that a society might supply in any combination. They are not separate, and the link between them cannot be severed.

I want to propose a different way of framing the whole subject, one that makes it measurable and, more importantly, makes the connection to justice explicit.

## The Reframing

Start from an observation that sounds harsh but is difficult to deny: **one person's freedom generally comes at the expense of another's.** Not always in a dramatic way, and not always deliberately, but structurally. Someone's freedom to pollute is someone else's loss of clean air. Someone's freedom to accumulate is someone else's narrower range of options.

If that is right, then you can build a model in which **the total quantity of freedom in a society is roughly fixed**, and the political question is not how much freedom exists but how it is distributed.

## Making It Measurable

The obvious objection is that "freedom" is too vague to quantify. But we can proceed by proxy.

**Measure it by free time.** Freedom is obviously not *only* free time, but free time is a decent index of it: the hours in which you are not obliged to anyone, in which your options are genuinely yours. Under this measure, the freedom in a society is the sum of all its free time — and that sum is very unevenly distributed.

**Or measure it by education.** The more educated a person is, the freer he is in a real sense: more options are visible to him, more are available, and more of what happens to him is intelligible. Also unevenly distributed.

Either proxy exposes the same structure. And notice that the proxies are not independent of money. More money buys more free time — directly, by letting you work less, and indirectly, by letting you purchase away the obligations that consume it. **Economic freedom converts into freedom in every other sense**, which is precisely why the two cannot be separated.

## Justice, Restated

Now the payoff. Under this framing, **justice becomes the achievement of equal resources measured in freedom** — not equal money, which was never the interesting target, but equal command over your own time and options.

That reframing does real work, because it tells you what justice is *not*.

Justice cannot mean the elimination of all exploitation. Exploitation happens constantly, in small amounts, everywhere. A butcher who overcharges you by a trivial sum has exploited you slightly. There is exploitation in marriages. There is exploitation in friendships. A programme aimed at eliminating all of it is not merely impractical; it misunderstands what it is looking at.

**The goal is to correct the large failures — the ones where exploitation is organised and where it costs a great many people a great deal of freedom.** The distinction is between background friction and structural extraction, and only the second is a political problem.

## Where the Correction Has to Happen

If that is the target, the level of intervention follows. Organised extraction operating at scale cannot be corrected at the level of the individual transaction, because the individual transaction is not where it lives. It has to be addressed at the highest political level — which is itself an argument for why direct democracy matters at that level specifically, rather than as a local or municipal curiosity.

The working image I keep returning to is hard kernels in soup. You are looking for the lumps: the places where a small group has taken a disproportionate share of the economic resources or the free time, or where it exploits the majority to its own benefit, and thereby holds more freedom than everyone else. Exploitation of the majority by a minority translates, in these terms, into **more freedom for the minority and less for the majority.** Same total, redistributed.

## The Obvious Objection, and the Answer

Here is the standard reply to any redistributive argument: break up that minority, and each individual gains only a *little* freedom. Divided among everyone, the gain per head is negligible. Why bother?

It is the same objection made against redistributing capital — spread it across the population and each person gets a small amount, so what was the point?

**The answer is that a small gain per head is enough, because the gain per head was never the objective.** Once the strata are more equal, you no longer have to work out what to do with the surplus at all. The problem the concentration creates is not that some people have too much stuff. It is that concentrated freedom purchases influence, sets the terms of everyone else's options, and reproduces itself. Break the concentration and that problem dissolves, regardless of how the arithmetic divides.

The size of the surplus was never the issue. The concentration was.
