Imagine a modern university announcing a Department of Astrology. Undergraduate degrees, a master's programme, doctorates in the interpretation of natal charts. The reaction would be instant and unanimous. The faculty senate would protest, the scientists would write scathing letters, the donors would withdraw. Nobody would need to spell out the justification, because it is obvious: astrology is not a field of knowledge. It has no empirical footing, no falsifiable claims, and no logical machinery capable of producing a valid conclusion.

Yet the same universities, all over the world, maintain well-funded faculties of theology, with endowed chairs and degree programmes, and nobody protests at all.

I want to argue that this is not an intellectual distinction. It is a historical accident protected by tradition. Epistemologically, theology stands on exactly the same ground as astrology, and neither of them belongs in an institution whose purpose is the pursuit of knowledge.

## Premises, Logic, Conclusions

Academic work rests on a simple compact. An argument begins from premises that are coherent and can be shown to hold, proceeds by sound logic, and ends in conclusions that can be evaluated, tested or contested against the world. Theology imitates this process. It borrows the vocabulary of scholarship and none of its structure, and it fails at all three stages.

**The premises cannot be examined.** In genuine scholarship a premise is empirically verifiable, mathematically demonstrable, or an axiom that everyone can see. Theology begins by assuming its conclusion: that supernatural beings exist, that a revelation occurred, that a text carries divine authority. A premise that cannot be checked, verified, or even clearly defined is not a premise. It is an assertion.

**The logic runs in a vacuum.** Theology is often praised for the intricacy of its internal debates, its scholastic distinctions and elaborate taxonomies. But complexity is not logic. An astrologer can compute, with real mathematical precision, the angular relationships between the planets and the arbitrary houses of the zodiac. Internal consistency inside an invented system is decoration. If the axioms do not connect to reality, every step that follows from them is meaningless, however rigorous the steps look.

**The conclusions cannot fail.** A real conclusion tells us something about the world that could, in principle, have turned out otherwise. In theology every outcome is fixed in advance by dogma. When a contradiction appears, the hypothesis is not revised; instead there is an appeal to mystery, to the ineffability of the divine, or to some interpretive acrobatics. A system in which no observation could ever prove a claim wrong is a system that cannot reach a true conclusion, because it has given up the only mechanism by which a conclusion gets to be true. I have written before about [why theology has never discovered anything](@/blog/why_theology_discovers_nothing.en.md); this is the reason.

## The Parallel, Attribute by Attribute

The university's refusal to host astrology shows up the double standard it grants theology. Put the two side by side:

| Attribute | Astrology | Theology |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foundational claim | Celestial bodies determine human destiny and character | Supernatural beings dictate cosmic and moral reality |
| Empirical basis | None; refuted by astronomical and psychological testing | None; immune to verification or testing by design |
| Method | Elaboration of ancient texts, hermeneutics, symbolic mapping | Elaboration of ancient texts, hermeneutics, dogmatic dialectic |
| Internal machinery | Elaborate rules, charts, aspects and classifications | Elaborate doctrines, apologetics and conceptual models |
| Standing in the university | Rejected everywhere as pseudoscience | Degrees, chairs, prestige and departmental funding |

If complexity, longevity and a textual tradition were enough to earn academic legitimacy, astrology would deserve a corridor next to astronomy. It has all three. The university is right to keep it out, and in keeping it out while welcoming theology it reveals that the standard being applied is not an epistemic one.

## Belief Is Not Knowledge

The root error is the conflation of belief with knowledge. Epistemology settled long ago that belief without justification and truth is not knowledge. Personal conviction, cultural inheritance and existential comfort are psychological facts about the believer. They are not intellectual achievements, and they say nothing about the world.

A university exists to preserve, discover and transmit knowledge. It is the place for disciplines that submit their hypotheses to critique, to empirical test and to the possibility of refutation. When it awards a credential, and above all a graduate credential, it is certifying that the holder has mastered an inquiry capable of extending human understanding. Awarding that credential in a field with no checkable premises, no logic that touches the world and no testable conclusions cheapens every other degree the institution grants. The doctorate in theology and the doctorate in physics hang on the same wall and carry the same title, and only one of them was earned by finding something out.

## Religion Can Be Studied, Theology Cannot Be Done

None of this says religion should not be studied. It should, and intensively. Religion as a historical phenomenon, a sociological one, a psychological one, a literary one, is an entirely legitimate object for departments of history, anthropology, sociology, psychology and literature. In those departments religion is examined from the outside, as a human construction, with the same tools applied to any other human construction.

Theology is not that. Theology does not study religion as an external object. It operates from inside the unverified assumptions of the dogma, taking as given exactly the things the other disciplines would set out to explain. The sociologist asks why people believe in resurrection. The theologian asks what resurrection means, having already assumed it happened. One of these is a research question. The other is devotional practice wearing a gown.

The academy's reluctance to say this out loud is of a piece with [its reluctance to blame religion for anything](@/blog/academic_cowardice_about_religion.en.md). But the principle is already accepted; it is only the application that is missing. We agree that astrology has no place in the university because it lacks evidence and logic. The same standard, applied honestly, evicts theology on the same day. It belongs in seminaries, in houses of worship and in private reflection, and in none of those places does it need a university's letterhead.
