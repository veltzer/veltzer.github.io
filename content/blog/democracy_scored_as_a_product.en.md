Suppose representative democracy did not exist and someone brought you its specification as a proposal. Not the ideals, the mechanism. Here is how the citizen's input into government will work; please review.

I find this a useful exercise because the mechanism is so familiar that nobody reads it any more. We argue about the people inside it and never about the spec. So here is the spec, one line at a time, with the review comments I would write in the margin.

## Line 1: Every Citizen Gets Exactly One Vote

Regardless of what he knows about the question, how much it affects him, or how much thought he has given it.

The defence of this line is equality, and I accept that part of it: any scheme that weights votes by some measure of competence has been tried and the measure was always chosen by the people it favoured. So equality of the vote is the correct default.

But notice what the line does not say. It does not say the citizen gets one vote *per question*. It says one vote, full stop, and the next lines explain what that single vote is spent on. Equality is the acceptable part of the line. The scarcity is smuggled in behind it.

## Line 2: The Vote Is Granted Automatically

Turn eighteen and it arrives. No preparation, no demonstration that you know what a budget is or what the office you are voting for actually does.

I am not proposing a test. Tests get captured. But look at what the automatic grant implies about the system's own view of the vote: **a thing you receive without doing anything is a thing the system does not expect you to do anything with.** We do not hand out driving licences this way, or the right to sit on a jury in a complex trial, because we expect those to be used. The vote is handed out like a receipt.

## Line 3: The Vote Applies Only to the Highest Offices

You vote for a legislator, a party, a president. You do not vote on whether the country goes to war, on what the tax rate is, on whether a given industry is regulated, on any of the thousand decisions those offices will take in your name.

A product review would flag this immediately. The customer is asked to choose a supplier and is then locked into whatever the supplier decides to deliver. Every question that matters is delegated wholesale, and the delegation is not revocable per item. I have [written before](@/blog/representative_government_altitude.en.md) about what this does to the debate; here I only note that no one designing a control system from scratch would give the operator exactly one knob and connect it to nothing in particular.

## Line 4: The Vote Is Cast Once in Several Years

Between elections the citizen has no input at all, and the system has no way of receiving any. Whatever happens in those years happens without him.

Compare this to any other feedback loop we rely on. A thermostat that read the temperature once every four years would not be called a control system. A company that consulted its customers once every four years would not have customers. **The sampling rate is so low that the signal is mostly noise**, which is exactly why elections turn on a gaffe, an attack, or the weather on the day.

## Line 5: There Is No Performance Review

The representative is not measured against anything. There is no metric he committed to, no target he can miss, no mechanism by which falling short costs him anything before the next election, at which point the question has been buried under four years of other questions.

The one review that exists, being voted out, does not distinguish between a representative who failed and one who was unlucky, and it does not apply to the great majority of legislators who sit in seats their party cannot lose. Every other position of comparable responsibility in society has an appraisal. This one has a popularity contest at long intervals.

## Line 6: The Majority Decides

Here the spec is honest about a real cost, and I want to be honest too. The majority is frequently wrong, and any system built on it inherits that.

But read the line in context. The spec does not let the majority decide *questions*. It lets the majority decide *who* decides questions, once, for years, without review. If you are worried about the stupidity of the majority, this is the arrangement that maximises its damage: the majority's one crude judgement is amplified into everything, and there is no later question on which it might correct itself.

## What the Review Comes To

Read as a specification, this is a control system with one input, granted without conditions, connected to nothing specific, sampled once every few years, with no feedback and no appraisal. **Nobody would propose it today.** Nobody would accept it for a thermostat.

It is accepted for government because it was not designed; it accreted, in an age when polling a nation took months and the alternative was a king. Both of those constraints are gone.

## What the Spec Should Say

The corrections are the obvious inversions. One vote per question rather than one vote per era. Votes on decisions rather than on personnel. A sampling rate measured in weeks, so that the signal is the public's view and not the day's news. And the only performance review that has ever worked: the decision itself is the public's, so there is no one to appraise and no one to blame but ourselves.

That is a description of direct democracy, and I did not arrive at it by ideology. I arrived at it by reading the existing spec and writing down what a reviewer would say.
